Tanja Clara:



4 years ago today I had a major surgery called a total laryngectomy and bilateral neck dissection as part of my treatment for a rare and locally advanced laryngeal cancer — adenoid cystic carcinoma.



This meant the removal of my larynx (my natural speaking voice gone forever), part of my trachea and thyroid, lymph nodes and other structures within my throat all gone. It’s a major and radical life-changing surgery which changed the way I breathe, eat, swallow and speak.



Having my natural voice taken away from me felt like being stripped of my very identity, my personality and my confidence. Our voices are so ingrained into who we are as a person. I still hear my old voice in my head and in my dreams all the time. I used to sing and I miss the loss of my singing voice hugely. 4 years on and the ache still feels as heavy in my heart and my throat as it did back then.



A large percentage of patients who go through a laryngectomy struggle with depression and anxiety following their surgery and it can make them withdraw from society, avoiding social occasions.



My children, above anything else, are the driving force and motivation for me to adapt and live and thrive with a laryngectomy. To be the confident, outgoing, fun and social mum that doesn’t let something like having a hole in my neck and an unusual voice hold me back.



There are many times that I feel self-conscious, anxious and nervous about my voice in public though. People can stare and sometimes ask inappropriate, insensitive and rude questions about my voice, so I have to do my best and appear confident and give off ‘I couldn’t give a shit vibes’.



4 years on I’m still learning to love my voice. On some days I really do, but there are days when it’s not working properly and it feels so effortless to speak. The skin around my stoma is sore, or I’m unwell, or my valve is leaking, or my reflux is really bad....



However, 4 years on, I’m also just really loving living my life. Life is too short not to, and life is for living.
